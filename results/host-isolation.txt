# Host-sensitivity check (manual, one-off)

Purpose: decide whether the multi-millisecond p99.9 tails in the matrix were a
property of the pipeline or of the host. This was a *diagnostic*, not part of the
committed measurement chain; it was performed once and not repeated.

Intervention: the desktop process responsible for the sustained load (a terminal
emulator, pid 43546, consuming ~380 % CPU for its entire lifetime) was confined to
the E-cores for the duration of the test with `taskset -a -pc 8-11`, and restored
to its original `0-11` affinity by a shell `trap` on exit. No process was killed,
reniced, or stopped.

Configuration under test in both halves: spsc / fast-slab / epoll / pinned engine,
1.01 M orders at 200 k orders/s, client pinned to CPU 6.

Both halves were measured while the desktop process was still running at ~380 % CPU.

| condition | n | p50 | p99 | p99.9 | max | net thread run-queue wait | orders >1 ms before parse |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| desktop free to use all CPUs | 3 | 26.0 us | 1046 us | 2400 us | 2896 us | 97.65 ms | 10017 |
| desktop confined to E-cores | 3 | 22.5 us | 68 us | 142 us | 240 us | 1.17 ms | 0 |

Same binary, same configuration, same client. The engine thread's run-queue wait
was 0.29 ms in the confined half, so the engine was not the delayed component: the
network thread was. A mutex-queue control measured in the same confined window
gave p99.9 = 299 us, which is consistent with the matrix's uncontended mutex rows
and shows the improvement was not an artefact of the queue type.

Interpretation: the multi-millisecond tails are ingress delay caused by host
scheduling contention, not by matching. This is the same conclusion the
`net_ingest_slow`, arrival/service split and `net run-queue wait` columns reach from
in-process counters alone, without touching the host.
